The Indian residential property market is characterised by heavy broker intermediation, which introduces opaque pricing, brokerage fees equivalent to one month's rent or one to two percent of the sale value, and information asymmetry that disadvantages both owners and seekers. NestQuest is a brokerage-free, self-service property marketplace developed as a web application to address these inefficiencies. Built on FastAPI with Jinja2 server-rendered templates, an asynchronous MongoDB Atlas database accessed via the Motor driver, and bcrypt-plus-JWT authentication, the platform allows property owners to post listings at no cost and prospective buyers or tenants to search, filter, shortlist, and send enquiries directly to owners without any intermediary. An admin moderation layer enforces listing quality through approval and featured-flag controls, while Cloudinary-backed image uploads, an SMTP enquiry-notification service, and a `/api/health` endpoint round out the operational surface. All sensitive credentials are externally configured, and passwords are hashed at bcrypt cost factor twelve with tokens transmitted via httpOnly cookies.

The platform is augmented by an AI price-estimator module built with scikit-learn Linear Regression, trained and evaluated on a synthetic dataset of 545 rows generated by a fixed linear formula with Gaussian noise, modelled on the structure of a public Kaggle housing dataset. Because the data-generating process is itself linear, the model achieves a coefficient of determination of R²≈0.65 on the held-out test set of 109 rows, with a Mean Absolute Error of approximately ₹7.58 lakh and an RMSE of approximately ₹9.39 lakh. These metrics reflect the constrained, synthetic nature of the training data and should be treated as a indicative proof-of-concept rather than a production valuation tool. The estimator is served live through a dedicated REST endpoint, predictions are logged to MongoDB for audit, and all known limitations — including the synthetic dataset, the modest R², and the absence of CSRF protection and regex-injection guards — are documented as directions for future enhancement.
